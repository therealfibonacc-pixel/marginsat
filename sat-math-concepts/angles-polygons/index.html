Angles, parallel lines, and polygons

Parallel lines, polygon angles and isosceles chains.

The key idea

With parallel lines cut by a transversal, angles are either equal or supplementary. Triangle angles sum to 180°; an exterior angle equals the two far interior angles. An n-sided polygon's interior angles sum to 180(n−2), and the exterior angles of any convex polygon sum to 360°.

The trap

Setting two angles equal when they're supplementary.

No graph will find an angle. Name the angle relationship, then solve.

Level 1 · Warm-up

What is the measure, in degrees, of each interior angle of a regular polygon with 10 sides?
Show the answer
Answer: 144The interior angles sum to 180(10−2)=1440 degrees, shared equally by 10 angles: 101440​=144. (Or: each exterior angle is 10360​=36, and 180−36=144.)

Level 2 · Test level

Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal. Two of the angles formed are same-side interior angles (on the same side of the transversal, between the parallel lines), with measures (7x−12)° and (3x+122)°. What is the measure, in degrees, of the larger of these two angles?
Show the answer
Answer: 143Same-side interior angles are supplementary: (7x−12)+(3x+122)=180. Solving gives x=7, so the angles measure 37° and 143°. The larger is 143°.

Level 3 · Beyond the test

In triangle ABC, AB=AC. Point D lies on BC so that AD=BD. If angle DAC measures 6°, what is the measure, in degrees, of angle ABC?
Show the answer
Answer: 58Let angle ABC=x. Then angle ACB=x (since AB=AC) and angle BAD=x (since AD=BD). Angle BAC=x+6, so (x+6)+x+x=180 and x=58.
