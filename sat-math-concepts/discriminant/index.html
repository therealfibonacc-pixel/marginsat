The discriminant: how many solutions

Counting solutions and finding tangent lines with b² − 4ac.

36practice questions
3levels

The key idea

For ax2+bx+c=0, the discriminant b2−4ac tells you the number of real solutions: positive means two, zero means one, negative means none. A line is tangent to a parabola when substituting gives a quadratic with discriminant 0.

The trap

Forgetting to move every term to one side before reading a, b, and c.

Can Desmos do it?

For a fixed equation, graphing shows the number of x-intercepts. When a constant is unknown, set the discriminant equal to 0 instead of hunting with a slider.

Level 1 · Warm-up

How many real solutions does the equation 4x2−3x+4=0 have?
  1. ANo real solutions
  2. BExactly one real solution
  3. CExactly two real solutions
  4. DInfinitely many solutions
Show the answer
Answer: AThe discriminant is b2−4ac=(−3)2−4(4)(4)=−55, which is negative, so the equation has no real solutions.

Level 2 · Test level

In the equation x2+kx+4=0, k is a positive constant. If the equation has exactly one real solution, what is the value of k?
Show the answer
Answer: 4Exactly one solution means the discriminant is 0: k2−4(1)(4)=0, so k2=16 and, since k>0, k=4.

Level 3 · Beyond the test

In the xy-plane, the line y=−3x+b, where b is a constant, intersects the parabola y=x2−7x at exactly one point. What is the value of b?
Show the answer
Answer: -4Set the expressions equal: x2−7x=−3x+b, or x2−4x−b=0. One intersection means the discriminant is 0: (−4)2−4(0−b)=0, so 0−b=4 and b=−4.
