Remainder and factor theorem

Remainders, factors and unknown coefficients.

36practice questions
3levels

The key idea

The remainder when p(x) is divided by x−a is p(a). So x−a is a factor exactly when p(a)=0. Each known remainder or factor gives one equation for the unknown coefficients.

The trap

Plugging in x=−a for the divisor x−a.

Can Desmos do it?

Desmos evaluates p(a) in a blink, but unknown coefficients still need equations from you.

What is the remainder when p(x)=3x3+3x2+6x−6 is divided by x−2?
Show the answer
Answer: 42By the remainder theorem, the remainder is p(2)=42.

Level 2 · Test level

The polynomial p(x)=x3+x2+kx+7, where k is a constant, has x+1 as a factor. What is the value of k?
Show the answer
Answer: 7Since x+1 is a factor, p(−1)=0: −1+1−k+7=0, so −k=−7 and k=7.

Level 3 · Beyond the test

When p(x)=x3+ax2+bx−3 is divided by x−2, the remainder is −1. When p(x) is divided by x+3, the remainder is −81. What is the value of a+b?
Show the answer
Answer: 1The remainder theorem gives p(2)=−1 and p(−3)=−81: 8+4a+2b−3=−1 and −27+9a−3b−3=−81. Solving: a=−4 and b=5, so a+b=1.
