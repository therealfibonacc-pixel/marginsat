Exponent rules and radicals

Fractional exponents, radicals and mixed bases.

36practice questions
3levels

The key idea

Multiplying powers adds exponents, dividing subtracts, and a power of a power multiplies. A radical is a fractional exponent: nxm​=xm/n. To compare different bases, rewrite them with one base first.

The trap

Adding exponents when raising a power to a power, or writing 16x−1 as 24x−1.

Can Desmos do it?

For an expression in x alone, graphing two forms shows whether they match. With two variables tied by a condition, rewriting the exponents is the only reliable route.

Level 1 · Warm-up

For x>0, the expression x8(x5)3⋅x1​ is equivalent to xk. What is the value of k?
Show the answer
Answer: 8A power of a power multiplies exponents: (x5)3=x15. Multiplying adds and dividing subtracts: 15+1−8=8.

Level 2 · Test level

For x>0, the expression 4x7​⋅x1 is equivalent to xk. What is the value of k?
Show the answer
Answer: 11/4Write the radical as a power: 4x7​=x7/4. Then add exponents: 47​+1=411​.

Level 3 · Beyond the test

If 2x−3y=−3, what is the value of 16x+14x+1⋅8y​?
Show the answer
Answer: 2Rewrite with base 2: 4x+1=22x+2, 8y=23y, 16x+1=24x+4. The total exponent is (2x+2)+3y−(4x+4)=−(2x−3y)−2=1. The value is 21=2.
