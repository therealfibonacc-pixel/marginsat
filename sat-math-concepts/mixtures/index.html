Mixtures and concentration

Concentration problems: mixing, adding water, draining and replacing.

36practice questions
3levels

The key idea

Track the amount of the pure substance, not the percent. Amount = concentration × volume. Mixing adds amounts and volumes; adding water adds volume only; evaporation removes water only; draining removes both in proportion.

The trap

Averaging two concentrations without weighting by volume.

Can Desmos do it?

After you write “acid before plus acid added equals acid after,” any solver finishes it. Writing that equation is the skill.

Level 1 · Warm-up

A chemist mixes 60 liters of a 5% salt solution with 40 liters of a 70% salt solution. What is the concentration of salt in the mixture, as a percent?
Show the answer
Answer: 31Salt: 0.05(60)+0.7(40)=31 liters, in 100 liters of mixture. The concentration is 10031​=0.31, or 31%. Simply averaging 5% and 70% ignores the different volumes.

Level 2 · Test level

How many liters of a 55% acid solution must be added to 30 liters of a 5% acid solution to make a 25% acid solution?
Show the answer
Answer: 20Acid before plus acid added equals acid after: 0.05(30)+0.55x=0.25(30+x). Then 0.3x=6, so x=20 liters.

Level 3 · Beyond the test

A 40-liter salt solution is 35% salt. How many liters of water must evaporate for the solution to become 65% salt?
Show the answer
Answer: 18.46Evaporation removes only water, so the salt stays at 0.35(40)=14 liters. At 65%, the total volume is 0.6514​=13280​ liters, so 40−13280​=13240​ liters evaporate.
